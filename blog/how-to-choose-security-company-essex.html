How to Choose a Reliable Security Company in Essex and London

With hundreds of security companies operating across Essex, London and the wider South East, selecting the right provider is one of the most important procurement decisions a business can make. Get it right and you have reliable, professional protection that gives you genuine peace of mind. Get it wrong and you could be paying for inadequate coverage, unvetted staff, or a provider that disappears when you need them most. Here are the ten questions that separate professional security companies from the rest.

1. Are All Officers SIA Licensed?

This is non-negotiable. Every officer performing a licensable security role — manned guarding, door supervision, close protection — must hold a valid SIA licence. Any company that cannot confirm this, or that hedges with "most of our staff are licensed," should be immediately disqualified. Ask for confirmation in writing and verify licences through the SIA's public licence checker before deployment begins.

2. Are Officers Vetted to BS 7858?

The British Standard BS 7858 defines the screening process for security personnel. It requires a minimum five-year background check covering employment history, address history, criminal records (enhanced DBS) and financial sanctions checks. A company that complies with BS 7858 has done the work to ensure its staff are genuinely trustworthy. Companies that cannot confirm compliance should be viewed with caution.

3. Is the Company Registered with the SIA Approved Contractor Scheme?

The SIA Approved Contractor Scheme (ACS) is an independently audited quality standard for security companies. ACS-approved companies have demonstrated compliance with industry standards in areas including governance, staff management, health and safety, and service delivery. While ACS approval is not mandatory, it is a meaningful indicator of a company's commitment to quality.

4. Do They Carry Adequate Insurance?

Any reputable security company will hold at minimum:

  • Public liability insurance — at least £5 million (£10 million is more appropriate for larger deployments)
  • Employers' liability insurance — legally required for any company with employees
  • Professional indemnity insurance — covering errors and omissions in service provision

Always request a copy of the current insurance certificate and verify it is genuine. Insurance gaps are a red flag for a company's overall professionalism.

5. What Is Their Response Time in an Emergency?

Established security companies should be able to commit to documented response times for emergency situations — whether that means additional staffing during an incident, emergency keyholding response or management support. Ask for their emergency escalation procedure in writing. A company that cannot answer this question clearly does not have a robust operational structure.

6. How Do They Manage Quality Control?

Quality control is what separates good security from unreliable security. Ask specifically:

  • How are officers supervised during shifts?
  • What is the process for reporting and escalating incidents?
  • How are unannounced quality checks conducted?
  • What happens if the assigned officer is unavailable?
  • Who is the point of contact for performance issues?

A good security company will have clear, documented answers to all of these questions.

7. Can They Provide References?

References from current or recent clients in a similar sector to yours are invaluable. A reputable company will be happy to provide references. When you speak to referees, ask not just whether they are satisfied overall, but specifically about how the company handled incidents, staffing gaps and complaints. The response to problems is often more revealing than the normal service.

8. What Are the Contract Terms?

Be very cautious of long minimum contract periods, automatic renewal clauses, and punitive exit penalties. The security industry has a poor reputation in this area, with some companies locking clients into multi-year contracts with very difficult exit routes. Look for:

  • Clear service level agreements with measurable standards
  • Reasonable notice periods (30–60 days is standard)
  • No automatic renewal without explicit consent
  • Clear definition of what constitutes a breach and your remedies

9. Do They Understand Your Specific Sector?

Security requirements differ significantly between a retail store, a construction site, a licensed venue and a corporate office. A security company that specialises in or has significant experience of your sector will understand the specific risks, regulatory requirements and operational constraints that matter to you. Ask for examples of similar clients they have worked with and what specific measures they implemented.

10. Are They Local and Responsive?

A security company with officers and management based in or near your area will be able to respond more quickly to emergencies, provide local knowledge that improves their effectiveness, and give you access to senior personnel when you need them. National companies with centralised management can struggle to provide the agile, responsive service that many businesses require.

Making Your Final Decision

Price matters — but it should not be the primary criterion. Security is one area where the cheapest option frequently proves the most expensive in the long run. A security failure — a theft, an assault on a staff member, a compliance breach — costs far more than the premium of a quality security provider. Evaluate on the full picture: licensing, vetting, insurance, references, flexibility and responsiveness.
