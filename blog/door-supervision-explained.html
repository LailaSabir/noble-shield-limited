Door Supervision Explained: What to Expect from Licensed Door Staff

The term "bouncer" conjures images of aggressive individuals standing at nightclub doors turning people away. The reality of modern, licensed door supervision in the UK is very different. Today's door supervisors are trained professionals holding a mandatory SIA licence, with skills ranging from conflict management to first aid. Here is what you should really expect from licensed door staff.

The Legal Requirement

Under the Private Security Industry Act 2001 and the Licensing Act 2003, any individual working as a door supervisor at a licensed premises in the UK must hold a valid SIA Door Supervisor licence. This applies at pubs, bars, clubs, music venues, concert halls and any other premises where alcohol is supplied under a premises licence.

Operating unlicensed door staff at your venue is a serious criminal offence. Licences are visible and must be worn while on duty. You can — and should — check the validity of any door supervisor's licence before they start their shift.

What Training Does an SIA Door Supervisor Receive?

Obtaining an SIA Door Supervisor licence requires completion of a regulated qualification — typically the Level 2 Award for Door Supervisors. This training covers:

  • Conflict management — de-escalation techniques, communication under pressure, managing aggressive behaviour
  • Physical intervention — lawful use of force, restraint techniques compliant with current legal and industry standards
  • Law and legislation — powers of arrest, search powers, licensing law, the Use of Force continuum
  • First aid — emergency first aid at work, including AED (defibrillator) use
  • Fire safety awareness
  • Drug awareness — recognition of drug use and impairment

This is a substantial training programme — typically 150+ guided learning hours. SIA door supervisors are genuinely skilled professionals, not simply large individuals on a door.

What Powers Does a Door Supervisor Have?

This is an area of frequent misunderstanding. Door supervisors do not have police powers. They are private citizens with exactly the same legal powers of arrest as any other member of the public — what is known as a "citizen's arrest" under Section 24A of the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984. They may only detain someone if they have committed (or are in the act of committing) an indictable offence, and only until the police arrive.

What door supervisors do have is the authority of the premises licence holder to refuse entry, request that individuals leave, and remove them from the premises using reasonable force if necessary. The emphasis is always on reasonable — proportionate to the situation.

Key principle: The goal of a professional door supervisor is always to prevent confrontation, not to engage in it. De-escalation is the first tool; physical intervention is always the last resort.

The Role of a Door Supervisor at Your Venue

Beyond the obvious function of managing entry and ejecting trouble-makers, a skilled door supervisor will:

  • Conduct age verification and ID checks professionally and consistently
  • Monitor the queue for signs of intoxication, aggression or impairment
  • Search attendees for prohibited items where your policy requires it
  • Liaise with bar staff and venue management to monitor the environment inside
  • Identify early signs of trouble and intervene before situations escalate
  • Manage ejections safely, lawfully and with minimum disruption
  • Maintain an incident log and provide detailed reports to management
  • Be the first point of contact in a medical emergency

How Many Door Supervisors Do You Need?

Your premises licence may specify minimum staffing levels — check your licence conditions first. As a general guide, for venues up to 200 capacity, a minimum of two door supervisors is recommended (never deploy a single door supervisor alone). For larger venues, one additional supervisor per 100 capacity is a reasonable baseline, adjusted for the risk profile of your venue and event.

Choosing the Right Door Supervisor Service

When hiring door supervisors, look beyond price. Ensure your provider:

  • Verifies and documents SIA licences before every deployment
  • Conducts enhanced DBS checks on all staff
  • Provides adequate briefing on your venue's specific policies
  • Has a clear escalation and incident reporting process
  • Carries adequate public liability insurance
