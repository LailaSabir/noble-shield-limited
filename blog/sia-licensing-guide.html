What Is SIA Licensing and Why Does It Matter?

If you have ever hired a security company or worked in the private security sector in the United Kingdom, you will have come across the term SIA licence. But what exactly is it, who needs one, and why should it matter to you as a business owner or client? In this guide, Noble Shield Limited's team of security professionals breaks down everything you need to know.

What Is the SIA?

The Security Industry Authority (SIA) is the organisation responsible for regulating the private security industry in the United Kingdom. Established under the Private Security Industry Act 2001, the SIA operates as a non-departmental public body sponsored by the Home Office. Its primary mission is to raise standards across the security sector, reduce criminality, and ensure that only properly trained, vetted individuals work in licensable security roles.

Who Needs an SIA Licence?

Under UK law, certain security roles require a valid SIA licence to operate legally. These include:

  • Door Supervisors — working at licensed premises such as pubs, clubs and bars
  • Security Guards — providing manned guarding at commercial or residential sites
  • Close Protection Officers — also known as bodyguards
  • Cash and Valuables in Transit Officers
  • Public Space Surveillance (CCTV) Operators
  • Vehicle Immobilisers
  • Key Holders

Operating in any of these roles without a valid licence is a criminal offence under the Private Security Industry Act 2001, carrying a fine of up to £5,000 and/or six months imprisonment.

What Does the Licence Check?

Obtaining an SIA licence is not simply a matter of paying a fee. Applicants must meet strict criteria, including:

  • Passing an identity check and a criminal record check (DBS)
  • Completing approved first aid training
  • Passing role-specific qualifications — for example, an Edexcel Level 2 Award for Door Supervisors or a Level 2 Award for Security Guards
  • Demonstrating the right to work in the UK

Certain criminal convictions will result in an application being refused, including offences involving violence, dishonesty or drugs.

How Long Does an SIA Licence Last?

SIA licences are valid for three years. Before expiry, licence holders must renew and demonstrate continued compliance with training and vetting requirements. The SIA maintains a public licence checker at their official website where anyone can verify whether an individual holds a current, valid licence — simply enter the licence number.

What Does an SIA Licence Look Like?

The SIA licence is a credit-card sized document bearing the officer's photograph, name, licence number, expiry date and the sector it covers (e.g. Door Supervision, Security Guarding). It features security measures to prevent forgery. Officers are legally obliged to wear or carry their licence while on duty and must present it on request.

Why Does SIA Licensing Matter to Your Business?

Hiring unlicensed security is not just dangerous — it exposes your business to serious legal and reputational risk. Here's why SIA licensing should be a non-negotiable requirement in any security contract:

  • Legal compliance — knowingly using unlicensed security staff may expose you to liability
  • Insurance validity — many business insurance policies require that contracted security staff are SIA-licensed
  • Quality assurance — licensing ensures a baseline of training and vetting
  • Peace of mind — you know exactly who is protecting your premises and that they have been vetted by an independent government body

How to Verify a Licence

The SIA provides a free public licence checker. Any person or business can enter a licence number to confirm it is valid and current. At Noble Shield Limited, we actively encourage clients to verify their assigned officers' licences. Transparency is a core part of how we operate.

Key takeaway: The SIA licence is the minimum standard of trust in the UK security industry. When hiring any security provider, always confirm that every officer deployed holds a current, valid SIA licence for the role they are performing.
